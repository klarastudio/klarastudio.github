    Orla Stories: Justin of valdah

    An interview I wrote for Orla Stories, the journal of Orla, a shop in Northampton. I also helped define the voice and vision for the publication: long-form conversations with the artists and makers whose work Orla carries, visited in their own studios.

    I visited Justin in his Northampton studio. His label, valdah, is named after his great-grandmother, a seamstress, and he makes clothes inspired by early and mid-century workwear on vintage machines, including a 1921 Singer 31 and a Wilcox & Gibbs chain-stitcher. We talked about mule ear pockets, wartime workwear, a 1950s Lee “beer jacket” and why clothes should be worn until they wear out.

    What I did: Interviewing and writing, helping define the publication’s voice and vision

  • Website · Art direction

    Syd White Portfolio

    A portfolio site for Syd, an event stylist who works mostly in fabric. Her professional work was mainly white weddings and corporate events, while her own eye runs richer and stranger, so the site needed to show both: a polished version of authentic.

    My starting point was that Syd’s work is made by hand and arranged in rooms, so the site should feel arranged too. To emphasize that, I drew on visual references I had shared in Unreal Estate, a course I developed: the film The Color of Pomegranates, with its walls of rugs and clustered objects, and Shaker gift drawings, where everything is arranged and floating on the page. I also looked to Sheila Hicks, Shona Heath and David Stark for their work with fabric and material. We steered away from pastels and anything florist-like toward deeper, jewel-toned and theatrical choices.

    The home page became an assemblage. Syd embossed the navigation herself in metal; I cut each plate out, colour-corrected them into one consistent metal and set them floating in a cloud-ringed sky, so the buttons feel like objects suspended in space rather than menu items. They lift and tilt when you hover, like something you could reach out and take. Inside, I treated her handmade decor the same way, each piece cut out and floating on white, and gave installations titles like Anthesis and Sea Change so they read as artworks. I also laid out a matching resume.

    What I did: Research, art direction, home page concept and build, image editing, page layouts, titles and copy, resume design

  • Community research · Wayfinding · Placemaking

    Downtown Orange, Massachusetts

    Two connected projects with one goal: encouraging people to gather in Orange’s downtown and to use and enjoy it.

    Wayfinding. Highway signs in the town centre pointed travellers somewhere else, so Orange felt like a place to pass through. Community surveys showed what residents loved: flight, from parachuting at the Jump Town airfield to the monarch butterfly migration, and the nickname “The Friendly Town.” That became a visual language of parachutes over Millers River, with identity guidelines, a sign family and location maps. Each sign is planted with butterfly milkweed, making them natural signs for nature’s wayfinders.

    Riverfront park. Teenagers already gathered on the old industrial riverfront. Instead of moving them on, the park plan starts from what was already happening there. I mapped how people used the site, documented the industrial artefacts and ran community surveys. The plan reuses machinery from nearby factories and adds a “star bar” for stargazing, a music stage, rain gardens and lighting.

    What I did: Community research and surveys, site mapping, visual identity, guidelines, signage design, park concept design

  • Engagement · Workshops · Media

    Valley on Board Transit Study

    A Federal Transit Administration HOPE grant project in western Massachusetts, reaching communities usually left out of transport planning, including Environmental Justice communities and rural residents.

    I designed engagement tools that made planning trade-offs feel concrete: a “would you rather” conversation card game and a polling system using custom “bus bucks.” Television appearances and media outreach brought in people who never come to public meetings.

    What I did: Engagement design, workshop facilitation, media outreach, presentation materials

  • Editorial · Brand identity · Events

    Housemates Magazine

    A magazine about living together: the housemate relationship, which is how many young people live, yet rarely gets told as a story.

    I collaborated on the logo, edited the magazine and co-led a speculative design workshop with Harvard Graduate School of Design’s Design Research Forum, asking architects and designers to draw what “home” means to them.

  • Publishing · Community

    OKAY Magazine

    A feminist publication and art collective I co-founded, designed, edited and published, made as an alternative to the slick look of commercial fashion magazines.

    Issues were organised around themes like “the color pink,” with a DIY zine aesthetic that welcomed imperfection. The community grew through printed issues, a website and an online reading group for bell hooks’ All About Love.

    What I did: Co-founder, art direction, design, editing, community building

  • Audio · Storytelling

    Low Tide Podcast

    A podcast I produced, co-wrote and co-hosted, treating the news as a beach to comb at low tide together rather than something to consume alone.

    It mixed reporting, conversation and narrative storytelling, including an episode on union organising at McLean Hospital. I composed the theme song in the style of a children’s TV show, a playful sound that told listeners this would be a different kind of political show.

    What I did: Producing, writing, hosting, theme song

  • Spaces · Visual identity

    The Laundry Room & Crush Galleries

    Two galleries made from overlooked spaces. At King’s College, students already gathered in the underground laundry room, so it became a gallery for feminist work. In Halifax’s North End, a vacant convenience store became Crush Gallery.

    Both kept the character of what the space had been. Crush’s hand-painted facade and logo grew from a soda can found on site.

    What I did: Concept, curation, identity, programming

A newsletter that celebrates the people in your community and helps the world of your work grow.
